Jamaica: Birthrate and the Economy

The birth rate in Jamaica has fallen below the recommended level and has been on the forefront of news paper and the government is advocating for women to get pregnant. They are aware that this is a socioeconomic issue, but they are not doing anything to solve this matter. The healthcare system is in shambles and the economy , well, is not so accommodating. Several issues have to be fixed before the birthrate in the country improves.

Background Information

In the 1970s, Jamaica's fertility was approximately 5.3 children per woman and the crude birth rate was about 33.79 per 1,000. By 2023, fertility had declined by roughly 65.6%. According to the International Journal of Economics & Finance Research & Applications, this shift coincided with substantial economic restructuring, social instability, and demographic transformation. GDP increased from about US$752 (1970) to US$7,299 (2024), suggesting uneven economic growth. The study notes that national fertility outcomes are also influenced by systemic issues such as violence, migration, mortality, economic growth, and employment patterns.

Causes

While reading the study, key findings were pointed out:

• Higher GDP is closely related to a lower birthrate

• Increased homicide rates lowers the birth rate

• Higher infant mortality predicts a higher birth rate

• High sucide rates are related to lower birth rates

Additionally, Sexual and Reproductive Health and Epidemiology Expert, Professor Affette McCaw-Binns, was quoted in a Jamaica Public Service article saying that education is a factor, as women are now focusing on finishing their education and delaying childbirth.

Some other factors I would add to this list as well includes:

Implications

A declining birth rate can have several significant impacts on a country. Some of these effects include an aging population, reduced labor productivity, lower national income, later retirement ages, and slower economic growth.

Actions Taken and Recommendations

So far, the Prime Minister of Jamaica, Andrew Holness, has called on members of the diaspora to return home to assist in addressing the country's declining birth rate. However, having more babies is not the sole solution because the environment they live in must be safe, and parents need to be in a position to raise children. An article titled Gov’t Crafting Plan to Tackle Declining Birth Rate quoted the Minister of Health, Dr. the Hon. Christopher Tufton, who mentioned that the government is looking at policies that encourage child-rearing and family formation, workplace nurseries and childcare support, strengthening maternal and neonatal services, and expanding community health support. International Journal of Economics & Finance Research & Applications research recommends that

1.Policies that promote education specifically womens and girls and improve access to family planning services

2. Imporve prenatal care, vaccination programs, nutrition, and sanitation and ensure that there is equal access to quality healthcare services especially in rural and underserved communities

3. More crime reduction strategies is needed
